The Price Reality

Tourist hotels are raising prices while fewer people want to stay in them. According to the first dataset from 2019 to 2025, titled Tourism Hotel Pricing and Occupancy Trends in Taiwan, the business model of local hotels faces a serious challenge.

The average price for a hotel room rose very quickly, jumping past NT$4,500 per night. For an ordinary family, this price is a heavy load. While room prices went up, the occupancy rate almost stopped growing, struggling to remain around 65%.

Abandoning Famous Scenic Spots

When hotel rooms cost too much, everyday people change their travel habits and stop visiting traditional scenic areas. The map visualization highlights the top 5 areas facing the sharpest decline in tourist arrivals.

  • 📍 Hsinchu County -49%
  • 📍 Kinmen County -36%
  • 📍 Lianchiang County -24%
  • 📍 Nantou County -22%
  • 📍 Hualien County -18%

The Official Numbers Game

These tourism trends do not fully match the government’s travel statistics. Comparing Domestic vs. Outbound Travel Growth shows very confusing numbers game.

The government index shows a giant 280% jump in domestic tourism in 2025, looking much better than outbound travel (110%). However, counting mobile phone signals that stay for over an hour automatically labels daily crowds in busy business areas as domestic tourists, making numbers appear higher than they actually are.

Data Methodology & Tracking

Tourism Data Processing Framework

Understanding the data compilation and the friction within official reporting guidelines.

Hotel pricing tracking metrics and regional tourism metrics were compiled utilizing performance parameters across domestic operators from 2019 to 2025. This allows for a direct comparison between market pricing behaviors and actual visitor volume trends.

To analyze macro trends, tracking datasets were correlated with official administrative policies, specifically the Guidelines for the Statistical Processing of Major Tourist and Recreational Sites (Effective 2024, Point VI).

Key Tracking Constraints & Technical Parameters:

• Technical Shift: The framework formally integrates telecommunications data collected via cell phone base stations to count crowds rather than relying strictly on manual physical headcounts.

• Inherent Biases: Official reports acknowledge these geolocation counts are systematically vulnerable to distortion by local terrain and fluctuating weather. The counting algorithm cannot differentiate native residents executing daily commutes or business appointments from real holiday vacationers.

• System Assumption: Mobile phone signals detected inside specific grids remaining static for over 60 minutes are classified automatically as domestic tourists, boosting official counts in dense business districts and city centers.
